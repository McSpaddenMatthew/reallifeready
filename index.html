Why reminders fail

More reminders are not the system.

Most capable kids do not need another adult saying, “Start your homework.” They need a clear process they can actually use when the adult is not sitting beside them.

When the process is missing

The danger is not one missing assignment. The danger is the pattern: the child waits, the parent reminds, the child resists, the parent rescues, and the child gets another day of practice depending on adults.

What the process has to cover

  • Knowing what needs to happen
  • Starting without a negotiation
  • Organizing materials
  • Transitioning off screens
  • Checking finished work
  • Resetting when something feels hard

Real Life Ready helps move the system from the parent to the child, one routine at a time.

The Real Life Ready Responsibility System

In 90 days, we build a responsibility system your child can practice.

The Roadmap tells us where your child is getting stuck. The 90-day coaching plan is where we build the systems, practice the routines, and support the parent side so the change can hold at home and school.

Days 1–30: Find the breakdown

We identify the main bottleneck and install the first system: starting homework, planning the week, managing screens, or resetting materials.

Days 31–60: Practice follow-through

Your child practices using the system with weekly coaching support, parent check-ins, and simple accountability.

Days 61–90: Transfer ownership

We reduce parent over-functioning and help your child take more responsibility for the routine without needing constant reminders.

Start System

How your child begins homework, chores, or routines without a parent sitting beside them.

Plan System

How your child sees the week before it becomes a crisis, breaks tasks down, and knows what needs to happen next.

Screen System

How your child transitions away from devices when homework, chores, bedtime, or family routines need attention.

Stuff System

Backpack, folders, laptop, charger, assignments, and the school/home setup that keeps materials from becoming the daily crisis.

Check-In System

How parent and child review what is due, what is next, and what is blocked without turning it into an argument.

Progress updates

Parents get plain-English feedback on what is working, where the child is still stuck, and what to practice before the next session.

Questions parents ask

Clear boundaries, practical support.

Is this executive function coaching?

Yes. Real Life Ready provides executive function coaching for practical daily skills: starting, planning, organizing, managing screens, and following through.

Is this therapy?

No. Real Life Ready provides coaching and practical skill-building. It does not diagnose or treat mental health conditions.

Is this tutoring?

No. The focus is not reteaching academic content. Schoolwork may be used as the training ground, but the goal is independence, organization, responsibility, and follow-through.

Do you work with teachers or counselors?

Yes. The work is designed to support the systems families already have. Teachers, counselors, therapists, doctors, and school teams all play important roles. Real Life Ready focuses on the practical implementation layer at home and school.

What happens on the free parent call?

We talk through what is happening, what you have already tried, and whether the assessment is the right next step.

Why is the assessment separate?

The Roadmap prevents families from jumping into coaching without first identifying the main problem. It creates the coaching plan and gives parents a clearer path.

What does the 90-day plan focus on?

The 90-day plan builds the Real Life Ready Responsibility System: the Start System, Plan System, Stuff System, Screen System, and Check-In System.

Is this only for kids with ADHD?

No. Some children have ADHD, some do not. Real Life Ready is for capable kids who need better systems for daily independence and responsibility.
