Why reminders fail

More reminders are not the system.

Most capable kids do not need another adult saying, “Start your homework.” They need a clear process they can actually use when the adult is not sitting beside them.

When the process is missing

The danger is not one missing assignment. The danger is the pattern: the child waits, the parent reminds, the child resists, the parent rescues, and the child gets another day of practice depending on adults.

What the process has to cover

  • Knowing what needs to happen
  • Starting without a negotiation
  • Organizing materials
  • Transitioning off screens
  • Checking finished work
  • Resetting when something feels hard

Real Life Ready helps move the system from the parent to the child, one routine at a time.

What happens if nothing changes?

Every day your child waits for you to start the process, the pattern gets more practiced.

This is not about perfection or blame. It is about practice. If the daily pattern stays the same, your child gets more practice depending on adults instead of more practice starting, planning, resetting, and following through.

The Real Life Ready 5-System Method

Five systems that make responsibility concrete.

Research-informed, teacher-built, and practice-based: executive function skills improve through explicit routines, repeated practice, feedback, and transfer across settings.

Start System

How your child begins homework, chores, or routines without a parent sitting beside them.

Plan System

How your child sees the week before it becomes a crisis, breaks tasks down, and knows what needs to happen next.

Screen System

How your child transitions away from devices when homework, chores, bedtime, or family routines need attention.

Stuff System

Backpack, folders, laptop, charger, assignments, and the school/home setup that keeps materials from becoming the daily crisis.

Check-In System

How parent and child review what is due, what is next, and what is blocked without turning it into an argument.

Weekly parent update

Parents get plain-English feedback on what we practiced, what changed, what still broke down, and what to practice before the next session.

What your child actually practices each week

This is not a lecture. It is coached practice with real responsibilities.

Each session turns the school and home responsibilities already causing friction into a clear routine your child can rehearse, explain, and repeat between sessions.

During a typical coaching session

  • 5 minutes: review what worked, what broke down, and what got avoided.
  • 10 minutes: inspect the planner, backpack, digital assignments, or current routine.
  • 15 minutes: build this week’s simple plan and identify the first three steps.
  • 15 minutes: practice one system: starting, planning, stuff, screens, or check-ins.
  • 5 minutes: have the child explain the plan back in plain English.
  • Weekly Action Plan: leave with the next three actions, the next reset routine, and the parent check-in question.

Real tasks we may practice

  • Open the assignment platform and identify what is due.
  • Write the first three steps instead of waiting for a parent to start it.
  • Reset backpack, folders, laptop, charger, and work space.
  • Plan the screen cutoff before the transition becomes a fight.
  • Rehearse the parent check-in so it sounds calm and specific.
  • Use a reset phrase when the work feels hard instead of shutting down.

Yes. Real Life Ready provides executive function coaching for practical daily skills: starting, planning, organizing, managing screens, and following through.
