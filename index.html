Founder note

Why I built Real Life Ready

I have spent years working with children who are bright, capable, and supported — but still struggle to manage the daily responsibilities school and life require.

I kept seeing the same pattern. The child could often do the work when an adult sat beside them. But they struggled to start, organize, plan, transition, and follow through independently.

That gap creates stress for the whole family. Real Life Ready was built to help close that gap with practical systems, parent support, and coaching that works alongside school teams — not against them.

Questions parents ask

Clear boundaries, practical support.

Is this therapy?

No. Real Life Ready provides executive function coaching and practical skill-building. It does not diagnose or treat mental health conditions.

Is this tutoring?

No. The focus is not reteaching academic content. The focus is starting, planning, organizing, managing screens, and following through.

Do you work with teachers or counselors?

The work is designed to complement teachers, counselors, therapists, doctors, and school supports — not replace them.

Will this work if my child resists help?

Often, yes — but the first goal is not to force buy-in. The first goal is to reduce friction, make expectations visible, and give the child a clearer path to success.

What if we have already tried therapy, tutoring, a 504, or school support?

Real Life Ready is designed to complement those supports. Many families have pieces of help in place but still need a practical implementation layer at home: routines, planning, organization, screens, and follow-through.

Is this just for kids with ADHD?

No. Some students have ADHD. Some do not. The program is for capable kids who need stronger systems for daily responsibility.

What if my child is smart but immature?

That is one of the most common fits. The goal is to help your child practice independence before the stakes get bigger.
